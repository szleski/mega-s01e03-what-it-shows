Two failure paths, two tickets

Shared parser. For day-first tenants, the shared parser assumes month first. This covers app and email reports, plus form reports through booking-v2. For example, R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July. R-41205 belongs here because Morrow Tax is configured as fr-FR, even though its browser reports en-US and intake guessed hint. The engineering update reports this path fixed by fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. The ticket retains the acceptance criteria for checking ambiguous day/month inputs, invalid dates, and month-first controls.

Wrong booking-v3 hint. For a day-first tenant using booking-v3, the form prints MM/DD/YYYY while interpreting the typed value using the tenant’s convention. Only the form channel takes this path. For example, R-41230 comes through Copper Studio’s booking-v3 form (es-ES), although intake calls it parser. The desk reports this path won’t fix because the booking-v3 form was retired. Retirement removes this reported entry path; it does not mean the hint was corrected. Changing the shared parser alone would not have corrected misleading form guidance.

These mechanisms come from the supplied executable model and tenant configuration. The export contains no production parser or form implementation to patch or independently reproduce.

The weekly plot uses receivedAt for arrivals and mergedAt for desk merges. Reports accumulated before the large July merge. An administrative burst must not be described as an incident onset. The export continues receiving reports after the bulk merge; the original page’s confident “one fix queued” wording hid a second workstream that later received a different disposition.

Triage by cause; keep unknown cases apart

The old triage page instructs the desk to merge matching subjects, but its own explorer does not implement that rule. Its review date is stale under the shared layout’s review policy. A familiar title and an accepted status do not make that advice current.

  1. Read the report and resolve its tenant, configured locale, form version, and channel.
  2. Compare the failure mechanism with an existing ticket. A matching cause can have different wording or arrive through another channel.
  3. Keep different causes apart even if their subject lines are identical. Form version and tenant configuration can change the classification.
  4. If the tenant or cause is unknown, investigate before merging. Unknown does not mean duplicate.
  5. Count one report per ID and preserve the intake label as a historical guess. Reconcile the split totals to the source ticket.
